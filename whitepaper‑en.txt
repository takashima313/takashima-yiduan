## I. Origin · The Prison Revelation

In the winter of 1859, the Edo prison was bitterly cold and damp. Takashima Kaemon, at twenty-seven years of age, imprisoned within those walls, chanced upon a copy of the *I Ching*. With no proper yarrow stalks at hand, he folded paper strips to serve as divination sticks and cast the oracle on the spot. The hexagram obtained was "Treading · The Well" *(履之井)* — the image revealed that a well-rope could be climbed, that salvation lay within reach. That very night, following the guidance of the hexagram, he scaled the prison wall along the well-rope and escaped to Yokohama, narrowly escaping death.

For the fifty-three years that followed, Takashima Kaemon's divinations never once failed. The Meiji Emperor summoned him privately on multiple occasions to inquire about the nation's fate. A single hexagram, "Waiting · The Well" *(需之节)*, accurately predicted the outcome of the First Sino-Japanese War — and every detail came to pass as foretold. When Itō Hirobumi sought his counsel before departing for Harbin, the hexagram obtained was "Keeping Still · Mountain" *(艮为山)*, an image of obstruction and warning. Takashima earnestly advised him to postpone the journey. Itō did not listen, and was assassinated upon arrival.

In his final days, Takashima wrote his own death date and memorial tablet inscription, and passed away peacefully on the very day he had foretold, at the age of eighty-three. The Emperor bestowed upon him the posthumous title: **Daikan'in Shin'eki Tōshō Koji** — "The Great Contemplator, Divine Diviner, Devourer of Images." The name of the Sage of Divination spread throughout the East and beyond.

Throughout his life, Takashima Kaemon used the *I Ching* to observe the rise and fall of the world and to discern fortune and misfortune for all who sought his counsel. His shrine, Shin'yidō (The Hall of Divine Divination), served both the imperial court and the common people — never once accepting payment, holding the four characters *Shisei Musoku* ("Sincerity Without Cease") as the sole guiding principle of his life and practice.

Today, we — a group of anonymous overseas open-source developers — offer this digital tribute to the Meiji-era Sage of Divination. We use code as yarrow stalks and open source as the vessel for transmission, digitally recreating Takashima's authentic simplified divination method. May the master's legacy of sincere divination endure forever in the digital world, built upon blockchain and open-source foundations.

### 3.1 Core Divination Algorithm: The Complete Authentic Takashima Simplified Method

The way of divination begins with a sincere heart. Sit quietly for a moment, compose your thoughts, set aside all distractions, and focus wholly upon what you seek to know.

Takashima Kaemon's Simplified Divination Method: Take fifty divination stalks. Set one aside to symbolize the Supreme Ultimate. Divide the remaining forty-nine into two to symbolize the Two Forms. For a man, the left hand determines the upper trigram; for a woman, the right hand. The count of the left hand, divided by eight, yields the upper trigram; the count of the right hand, divided by eight, yields the lower trigram. Divide the forty-nine once more, and divide the count by six to determine the moving line.

The hexagram is summoned by the sincerity of your mind — it is the oracle of the present moment. To encounter the inauspicious is not a fixed fate; by discerning gain and loss, one may turn away from misfortune and toward good fortune. To obtain the auspicious is not a permanent possession; only through steady self-possession can one preserve good fortune.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**

1. **Establish the Taiji (Supreme Ultimate) and Base Number**  
   Take a complete set of fifty virtual yarrow stalks. Set one stalk aside to symbolize the Supreme Ultimate, unmoving. The remaining forty-nine stalks serve as the base number for divination.

2. **First Division — Determine the Upper Trigram**  
   Randomly divide the forty-nine stalks into two groups. Following the principle of "male left, female right," take the count from the corresponding hand. Divide this number by eight. The remainder determines the upper trigram (a remainder of zero corresponds to the Kun hexagram).

3. **Second Division — Determine the Lower Trigram**  
   Randomly divide the forty-nine stalks into two groups once again. Following the same principle, take the count from the corresponding hand. Divide by eight. The remainder determines the lower trigram (zero corresponds to Kun).

4. **Third Division — Determine the Single Moving Line**  
   Randomly divide the forty-nine stalks for a third time. Following the same principle, take the count from the corresponding hand. Divide by six. The remainder determines the moving line (zero corresponds to the top, sixth line).

### 3.2 Takashima's Distinctive Rule: Single-Moving-Line Divination

This project strictly adheres to the "single moving line" system that Takashima consistently used after his middle years. Each divination generates only one changing line. This approach avoids the confusion caused by multiple moving lines, which can muddy the interpretation and obscure the meaning. It aligns perfectly with the minimalist spirit of the Simplified Method, ensuring that the interpretation is clear, direct, and the judgment of fortune and misfortune is unmistakable.
